How to Care for a Cyclamen (and Keep It Flowering)

Home › Flower Notes › Cyclamen Care

By Lynn20 November 20267 min read

From autumn through to spring, the plant I recommend most in the shop is the cyclamen. It flowers for months through the darkest part of the year, in whites, pinks and deep reds, and it makes a lovely Christmas gift that lasts far longer than a bunch of flowers.

But I also hear "mine died within a fortnight" more than I'd like. Almost every time, it's down to the same two things: the plant was kept too warm, or it was watered from the top. Get those right and a cyclamen is one of the easiest plants you can own.

The Quick Answer

  • Keep it cool: 10–15°C is ideal. A cool, bright windowsill, never next to a radiator.
  • Water from below: stand the pot in a saucer of water for 20 minutes, then tip away what's left.
  • Water only when it's dry: usually every 5 to 7 days in a cool room.
  • Pull off faded flowers: twist and pull the whole stalk from the base, and new buds keep coming.
  • Feed every two weeks while it's flowering, with a half-strength liquid plant food.

Looked after like this, an indoor cyclamen will often flower from November right through to March or April.

Where to Put It

Cyclamen grow wild on cool, shady hillsides around the Mediterranean, where they flower through the mild, damp winter and rest in the summer heat. Indoors, they want the same thing: cool and bright.

Spot How it does
Cool bedroom or spare room windowsillIdeal. Flowers for months.
Porch, conservatory or hallway (frost-free)Very good, as long as it doesn't freeze.
Kitchen windowsillFine if it's away from the oven and kettle steam.
Warm living roomShort-lived. Expect a few weeks, not months. Move it somewhere cooler at night if you can.
Above or next to a radiator or fireAvoid. The leaves yellow and the flowers collapse within days.

Give it plenty of light, but keep it out of strong direct sun, especially through south-facing glass. In a Yorkshire winter that's rarely a problem.

A cool, bright windowsill, well away from the radiator, is the best spot in the house.

How to Water a Cyclamen

This is where most cyclamen go wrong. The plant grows from a flat, round tuber that sits at the top of the compost, and if water sits on top of it, it rots.

  1. Check first. Push a finger into the compost. If it's dry an inch down, or the pot feels light when you lift it, it's time to water. If it's still damp, wait.
  2. Water from below. Stand the pot in a saucer or bowl of water for about 20 minutes, so the compost can soak it up from the bottom.
  3. Tip away what's left. Never leave it standing in water, or the roots will rot.
  4. If you must water from the top, pour it carefully around the edge of the pot, never onto the crown in the middle where the stems meet the tuber.

In a cool room that usually means watering every 5 to 7 days. In a warmer room it will dry out faster, so check more often.

Twenty minutes in a saucer of water, then tip away the rest.

Keep It Flowering

  • Pull off faded flowers and yellow leaves. Hold the stalk low down, give it a gentle twist and pull it away from the tuber. Don't snip it, because a leftover stump can rot. The plant then puts its energy into the buds waiting underneath the leaves.
  • Feed it every two weeks while it's in flower, with a liquid plant food for flowering plants at half the strength on the bottle.
  • Keep it cool at night. If it lives in a warm room during the day, moving it to a cold room or porch overnight makes a real difference.

Twist and pull faded stalks right from the base, and more buds will follow.

What's Wrong With My Cyclamen?

  • The whole plant has flopped. It's almost always thirsty. Stand it in a saucer of water for 20 minutes and it will usually perk up within a few hours.
  • It's floppy but the compost is wet. It's been overwatered or it's too warm, and the roots or tuber may be rotting. Let it dry out and move it somewhere cooler. If the tuber feels soft and mushy, sadly it won't recover.
  • The leaves are turning yellow in winter. Usually too warm, too much water, or both. Remove the yellow leaves and move it somewhere cooler.
  • Grey fuzzy mould on the stems or flowers. That's botrytis, caused by damp, still air and water on the crown. Remove the affected parts, water only from below and give it a little more air.
  • The flowers have stopped. It's probably too warm or too dark. Move it to a cooler, brighter spot, keep pulling off faded stalks and start feeding it.

Can Cyclamen Go Outside?

The cyclamen we sell as houseplants are not frost-hardy. They're perfect in a sheltered porch or a cool conservatory, but a hard frost will kill them.

The smaller "mini" cyclamen are often planted in outdoor pots and window boxes for winter colour, and they cope well with cold, wet weather in a sheltered spot. In a sharp frost, move them against the house wall or into a porch overnight.

There are also truly hardy garden cyclamen, with smaller flowers, that come back every year under trees and shrubs. They're a different plant from the indoor kind.

After It Finishes Flowering

In spring, your cyclamen will slow down and its leaves will start to yellow. This is normal. It's going into its summer rest, not dying. To keep it for next year:

  1. Gradually water less as the leaves yellow, and stop feeding.
  2. Let the leaves die back, then put the pot on its side somewhere cool and shady, such as a shed shelf or under a garden bench, and keep it almost dry over the summer.
  3. In late summer, when you see new growth, stand the pot upright, start watering again from below and bring it back indoors before the first frosts.

It doesn't always work, and plenty of people simply enjoy their cyclamen for the winter and buy a fresh one next autumn. Either is fine.

Choosing a Healthy Cyclamen

  • Look under the leaves for plenty of tight buds. That's where the next few months of flowers are coming from.
  • The leaves should be firm and dark green, with no yellowing or grey mould.
  • The plant should feel firm in the pot, with no soft, mushy patches around the base of the stems.
  • Wrap it for the journey home. They don't mind the cold, but icy wind can damage the flowers.

Lots of tight buds under the leaves means months of flowers to come.

Pets and Cyclamen

Cyclamen are harmful to cats and dogs if they're eaten. The tuber is the most toxic part, so keep pots out of reach, especially if you have a dog that likes to dig. If you think your pet has eaten some, call your vet.

Common Questions

Where is the best place to put a cyclamen?

Somewhere cool and bright, ideally between 10 and 15°C, out of direct midday sun and well away from radiators, fires and the oven. A windowsill in a cooler room, a porch or an unheated spare room is perfect. A warm living room is the quickest way to shorten its life.

How often should I water a cyclamen?

Water when the compost feels dry an inch down and the pot feels light, which is usually every 5 to 7 days in a cool room. Water from below: stand the pot in a saucer of water for about 20 minutes, then tip away what's left. Never pour water onto the crown in the middle of the plant.

Why are my cyclamen leaves turning yellow?

In winter, it's usually because the room is too warm or the plant is being overwatered. Move it somewhere cooler and let it dry out a little between waterings. In late spring, yellowing leaves are normal: the plant is going into its summer rest.

Why is my cyclamen drooping?

A cyclamen that flops suddenly is usually thirsty. Stand it in a saucer of water for 20 minutes and it will often perk up within a few hours. If the compost is already wet and it still droops, it may be too warm or the roots may be rotting from overwatering.

Are cyclamen poisonous to cats and dogs?

Yes. All parts are harmful if eaten, and the tuber (the bulb-like root) is the most toxic part. Keep cyclamen out of reach of pets, especially dogs that like to dig in pots.
