Christmas Flower Gifts: What to Send Hosts, Teachers & Neighbours

Home › Flower Notes › Christmas Flower Gifts

By Lynn20 November 20267 min read

December is the month of little thank-yous: to the friend hosting Christmas dinner, the teacher who's looked after your child all term, the neighbour who takes your parcels in. Flowers are one of the easiest and most welcome ways to say it, as long as you choose the right thing for the person. A big loose bouquet handed to a host with a houseful of guests isn't quite the gift it should be.

Here's what we suggest in the studio for each person on your list, plus what to write in the card and when to order.

For the Host: Choose Something Ready to Display

This is the one tip we give more than any other. When you arrive at a Christmas party with a bouquet, the host has to stop greeting guests, find a vase, trim the stems and find somewhere to put it, usually in a kitchen that's already full. It's kindly meant, but it's one more job.

An arrangement that's already in water solves that. A Christmas hat box (medium from £45, large from £60) or a small table arrangement can go straight onto a sideboard or windowsill, looks lovely all evening, and keeps going for a week or more afterwards. If you'd still like to give a bouquet, ask for it to be delivered the day before instead, so it's settled in a vase before anyone arrives.

Hosting yourself this year? Our guide to making a Christmas table centrepiece shows you how to dress the table.

A hat box goes straight onto the sideboard, with no vase to find in the middle of the party.

For a Teacher: Small, Simple and Long-Lasting

Teachers are given a lot of gifts in the last week of term, and they have to carry them all home. The best flower gifts are compact and easy to transport, and they'll last through the holidays rather than fading in an empty classroom.

  • A small festive pot plant is the easiest choice of all. Lynn makes little potted plants dressed for Christmas for around £10, so they're an easy, affordable thank-you from one family.
  • A cyclamen is our favourite. It flowers for weeks in a cool room, comes in festive reds and whites, and is very easy to look after (see the care tips below).
  • A small hand-tied bouquet in water travels well in a car and needs no arranging.
  • Give it a few days early. Taking it in a day or two before term ends means it isn't lost in the last-day rush, and there's a better chance it'll go home rather than stay in school over the holidays.

If several parents want to club together, one larger arrangement from the whole class is often nicer than lots of little gifts.

A cyclamen is easy to carry home and flowers for weeks in a cool room.

For Neighbours, Friends and Colleagues

  • A small fresh wreath (our 8 inch fresh wreaths start at £10.50) is a lovely way to thank a neighbour. It goes straight on their door and lasts right through the season. Our wreath size guide explains which size suits which door.
  • A flowering plant such as a cyclamen suits almost anyone, and it doesn't need a vase.
  • A festive trough of foliage and berries (medium from £35) is ideal for a windowsill or an office reception. For gifts for a whole team or a client list, see our guide to corporate Christmas flowers.

For Family You Won't See This Christmas

If you can't be together this year, flowers delivered to the door are the next best thing to being there. A Christmas bouquet (from £25) of reds, whites and winter greenery, or a hat box that's ready to display, arrives fresh and hand-made, with your message in it.

Try to have it arrive a few days before Christmas rather than on Christmas Eve, when everyone's busy and often out. And if they'll be away over Christmas itself, send them before they go. Our guide to flower delivery in Huddersfield and Barnsley covers the areas we deliver to.

For Someone Finding Christmas Hard

For anyone spending their first Christmas after losing someone, the season can be very difficult. Flowers are a quiet way to let them know you're thinking of them. Choose soft whites, creams and greens rather than bright reds and glitter, and keep the card simple. Something like "Thinking of you this Christmas" says a great deal.

Plants That Make Lovely Gifts

A plant keeps on giving long after the cut flowers have gone, and it's a gift that doesn't need any arranging at all.

  • Cyclamen. Our top pick for Christmas. Keep it somewhere cool and bright, away from radiators, and water it from below: stand the pot in a saucer of water for 20 minutes, then let it drain. Pull off faded flowers with a gentle twist. Looked after like this, it'll flower for weeks. Our cyclamen care guide has the full detail.
  • Poinsettia. The classic Christmas plant, but it hates cold draughts, so it needs wrapping well for the journey home. See our poinsettia care guide.
  • A Christmas pot. For a front door or porch, a pot of winter foliage, berries and pinecones (large from £45) lasts all season outdoors.

For more easy plants, see our houseplant care guide for beginners.

What to Write in the Card

Keep it short and from the heart. A few ideas:

  • For a host: "Thank you for having us, it wouldn't be Christmas without you."
  • For a teacher: "Thank you for a wonderful year. Have a lovely, restful Christmas."
  • For a neighbour: "Thank you for all the parcels! Merry Christmas from number 12."
  • For family far away: "Wish we could be there. Sending all our love this Christmas."
  • For someone grieving: "Thinking of you this Christmas, with love."

Always put your name (and surname, if the card is going to a school or office). It's surprising how many gifts arrive with just "From us"!

A short, personal message is all a card needs.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Lilies for someone with a cat. Every part of a lily is very poisonous to cats, even the pollen and the water in the vase. If you're not sure, leave them out. Roses, carnations and spray chrysanthemums are safer choices.
  • Forgetting about dogs and children. Holly berries, mistletoe and ivy are harmful if eaten, so they're best kept out of reach.
  • Strong scents for someone unwell or with allergies. Choose unscented, low-pollen flowers instead.
  • Leaving it until Christmas week. The most popular arrangements book up first, and deliveries get very busy.

When to Order

As early in December as you can, and at least a week ahead for anything you need in Christmas week. For a party, ask for delivery the day before. For teachers, order for the last few days of term. Common Questions

What flowers make a good gift for a Christmas host?

An arrangement that's ready to display, such as a hat box or a small table arrangement, rather than a loose bouquet. The host can simply set it down, without having to find a vase and trim stems while their guests are arriving.

Are flowers a good Christmas gift for a teacher?

Yes. A small, long-lasting gift works best, such as a little festive pot plant (ours are around £10) or a cyclamen. Give it a few days before the end of term, so the teacher isn't carrying armfuls of gifts home on the last day.

What Christmas flowers are safe for homes with cats?

Avoid lilies completely, as every part of the plant, including the pollen and the water in the vase, is very poisonous to cats. Roses, carnations and spray chrysanthemums are safer choices, and holly, mistletoe and ivy are best kept out of reach of cats and dogs.

When should I order Christmas flower gifts?

As early in December as you can, and at least a week ahead for anything you need in Christmas week. For a party, ask for delivery the day before, so the flowers have settled and the host isn't interrupted on the day.

What should I write on a Christmas flower card?

Keep it short and personal: who it's from, plus a line of thanks or good wishes. For example, "Thank you for having us, merry Christmas" for a host, or "Thank you for a wonderful year" for a teacher.
